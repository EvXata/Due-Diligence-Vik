# MBB Strategy Framework — 5 Lenses
*Extracted from MBB Strategy Framework document (MBB, June 2001)*

## The Four Strategic Pillars

Every MBB strategy engagement rests on four interlocking questions:

1. **Competitive Advantage** — What allows this business to generate returns above cost of capital?
2. **Business Segmentation** — In exactly which segments does the company compete, and are those segments defensible?
3. **Customer Needs** — What do customers need, and what is unmet?
4. **Segment Growth** — How fast are the relevant segments growing, and what does that mean for competitive positioning?

These four pillars are assessed through **five sequential lenses**.

---

## The Five Lenses

```
Description → Advantage → Future → Options → Selection
(Understand sources of advantage)    (Evaluate strategies)
```

The flow is logical, not chronological — parallel processing is the norm.

---

## Lens 1: Description Lens

**Question:** What business segment are we in, and is it attractive?

**Key outputs:**
- First-cut definition of the relevant business segment
- High-level view of competitive landscape
- Understanding of profitability and return patterns
- Detailed understanding of the business unit

**Key questions to answer:**
- What business segment are we in? What are related/adjacent segments? Where is the "white space"?
- What is the business unit (defined by limited corporate parent effect)?
- What are the economics of the segment?
- What is the pattern of profitability across competitors?
- Is the business segment attractive, and how is attractiveness changing over time?
- How do parts of the BU contribute to profitability?
- Is the BU gaining or losing relative share/profitability?

**Business segment definition rules:**
- A segment exists where a competitor can exist profitably WITHOUT participating in adjacent segments
- Means: little cost sharing between adjacent segments AND/OR costs are largely segment-specific
- Test: can a competitor in this segment ignore adjacent segments and still win?

**Analytical tools:**
- Industry lifecycle curve (profit vs. time)
- Competitor profitability analysis (ROCE, TSR, P/E)
- Value chain analysis (where is value created vs. captured)
- DuPont analysis (ROE decomposition)
- Market share analysis over time (gaining/losing)
- Five Forces analysis: competitors, customers, suppliers, substitutes, barriers to entry
- BU cost structure breakdown
- Revenue driver analysis (price × volume decomposition)
- Profitability by segment (geography, product, channel, customer type)

## Lens 2: Advantage Lens

**Question:** Why is the current winner winning? Is our BU advantaged or not?

**Key outputs:**
- Verdict on whether this is a **cost game**, **user value game**, or **both**
- Specific, detailed list of current sources of advantage and BU's position on each
- Assessment of what capabilities drive competitive advantage
- Defensibility and sustainability of advantage

**Cost game vs. User value game:**
- **Cost game signals:** Little price differentiation; little difference in product/service offering
- **User value game signals:** Variance in price realization; strong customer preference; clear product differentiation; strong relationship component; heavy sales/marketing spend

**Sources of cost-based advantage (MBB taxonomy):**
- **Scale:** Total volume → experience curve; scope (platforms); focus (limiting SKUs/value chain/customer segment = reducing complexity costs)
- **Process effectiveness:** Working capital, overhead, distribution, technology
- **Factor costs:** Labor, raw materials, cost of capital, cost of information
- **Heritage:** Regulation/patents, access to natural resources

**Sources of user value-based advantage (MBB taxonomy):**
- **Tangible differentiation:** Product quality (design, features, performance, reliability, ease of use); impact on user's systems (cost reduction, value capture, switching costs); customer service (policies, personnel, delivery, convenience); breadth of product range; differentiated pricing
- **Intangible differentiation:** Access (shelf space, channel range, navigation); relationships (trade, customer); brand (consumer franchise, image, prestige)

**Three levels of competitive advantage:**
1. **Nature of advantage:** Is the BU capturing the benefits? (price premium, volume premium)
2. **Sources of advantage:** How is the BU advantaged? (quality, scale, brand)
3. **Drivers of advantage:** Why? (what creates and sustains the sources)

**Drivers of sustainable competitive advantage:**
1. **Unique resources:** Only one player can possess (patents, unique locations) → cannot be replicated
2. **Scarce resources / critical mass:** Prohibitively expensive to replicate (scale, experience curves, network effects)
3. **Capabilities-based:** Embedded in complex organizational structure — competitor may not understand the source, cannot buy it, cannot build it in time, or circumstances were unique

**Analytical tools:**
- Scale curve (unit cost vs. cumulative volume)
- Experience curve (unit cost vs. elapsed time)
- Supply curve (cost position of all players)
- Price premium analysis
- Customer segmentation analysis
- Conjoint analysis / customer discovery
- Switching cost analysis
- Cost benchmarking
- Activity-based costing
- Revenue driver analysis

## Lens 3: Future Lens

**Question:** How will the competitive landscape change, and who will win tomorrow?

**Key outputs:**
- Overall verdict on stability (stable / evolutionary / discontinuous)
- Specific understanding of whether the situation is: low-growth, high-growth, or new game
- View on how business segmentation may change
- Hypothesis on future competitive positions
- Quantified predictions of impact of likely changes

**Two types of change:**
- **Evolutionary change:** Industry-driven, gradual — shifting market shares, high market growth rates, new ways of meeting customer needs
- **Discontinuous change:** Externally-driven, step-change, disruptive — technology, regulation, information economics ("Deconstruction": disassembling traditional value chains allowing new leaders to emerge)

**Key questions:**
- Will the definition of the business segment change (convergence, deconstruction)?
- Will sources and drivers of advantage change (technology disrupts cost structure, customers change needs)?
- Will the winners change?
- Are there significant unmet customer needs that could fuel disruption?
- How sustainable are current advantages?

**Future lens inputs from prior lenses:**
- Historical market growth rates
- Links to / potential for substitution by adjacent segments
- Development and growth of specialized sub-segments
- Trends in profitability and market share of main players
- Historical evolution of customer needs
- Level of current customer compromises (where are customers forced to accept less than ideal?)
- Understanding of competitor capabilities and strategies

**Analytical tools:**
- Adoption curve / industry lifecycle
- SPI (Strategic Positioning Index) environments
- PEST analysis
- Scenario planning
- Industry volatility analysis
- Analogies from other business segments that have undergone similar evolution

## Lens 4: Options Lens

**Question:** What realistic strategies exist, and what are their expected outcomes?

**Key outputs:**
- Realistic options quantified by: volume, cost, price, profit, value created, level and sustainability of advantage

**The Strategy Pattern Diagnostic (2×2):**

**Question 1:** Is the BU winning the current game?
- How does BU profitability compare with competitors?
- Is the BU advantaged or disadvantaged on sources and drivers of competitive advantage?

**Question 2:** Is the game stable?
- Evolutionary change? (growth, dissatisfactions)
- Discontinuous change? (technology, regulation, information economics)

**Result → Three pattern types:**

### Pattern A: Low Growth Situation
(Stable game, mature market)

*If winning:*
- Build on advantage: fortify by acquiring capabilities/competitors; grow business segment; expand into related adjacent segments
- Exploit advantage: profit from leader position; look for ways to change the game

*If losing:*
- Profit improvement with little growth or investment
- Shrink business to more attractive segments
- Focus on better meeting needs of specific customer groups
- Possibly: re-segment and look for growth sub-segments

### Pattern B: High Growth Situation
(Growing market, evolutionary dynamics)

*If winning:*
- Invest aggressively to grow faster than market (organically + M&A)
- Leverage capabilities into adjacent high-growth segments
- Maximize "option value" of position

*If losing:*
- Use growth to attack market leader through differentiation/focus
- "Sell your space" at a premium if advantage is not achievable
- Look to change the game

### Pattern C: New Game Situation
(Discontinuous change underway)

*If winning:*
- Create options by investing in new assets/capabilities
- Acquire/partner to obtain new assets/capabilities
- Protect original business by segmenting and focusing

*If losing:*
- Maximize value of current business by divesting at premium to new winners
- Become a winner in the new game by partnering or acquiring new capabilities/assets
- Divest

**Generating and quantifying options:**
Combine outputs of prior 3 lenses with strategy patterns AND:
- Evolutionary and discontinuous forces of change
- Current strategy and vision
- Opportunities to undermine competitors
- Opportunities from industry analogies
- Partnership/alliance opportunities
- Attractiveness of specific business segments

## Lens 5: Selection Lens

**Question:** Given uncertainty, which option is best?

**Expected value alone is insufficient** — must assess variability of returns.

**Three risk dimensions:**

### Competitor behavior risk
- What will competitors do proactively (ahead of our moves)?
- What will competitors do reactively (in response to our moves)?
- What would the "killer" competitor look like?
- Tools: Game theory, growth-growth matrix, competitive scenario trees

### Exogenous risk
- Changes in market growth rates
- Macroeconomic changes
- Changes in factor costs
- Timing of discontinuous events
- Tools: Scenario planning, probability distribution of returns, sensitivity analysis

### Implementation risk
- Organizational capability to execute
- Time required vs. market window
- Resource requirements and availability
- Cultural/change management barriers

**Output:** Recommended strategy with explicit probability distribution of returns, contingent strategies for downside scenarios, and clear decision criteria.

## Key MBB Concepts Summary

### MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)
Every breakdown — segments, options, advantages, next steps — must cover 100% of the space with zero overlap.

### Pyramid Principle
Lead with the conclusion. Support with 3 arguments. Support each argument with data. Never bury the "So What."

### So What Test
Every data point must answer: "And therefore what does management need to do differently?"

### Hypothesis-Driven Approach
Form specific, testable hypotheses BEFORE collecting data. Data collection is targeted to confirm or reject, not to explore randomly. Speed comes from focus.

### MBB Growth-Share Matrix
```
                   HIGH relative share  |  LOW relative share
HIGH market growth |    STARS ⭐        |  QUESTION MARKS ❓
                   | Invest to grow     |  Invest or exit
───────────────────┼────────────────────┼────────────────────
LOW market growth  |   CASH COWS 🐄    |     DOGS 🐕
                   | Extract cash       |  Divest/harvest
```

Relative market share = company share ÷ largest competitor's share
Market growth threshold: typically 10% YoY

### The Growth Trap
"Growth without advantage can be a cash trap" — high growth markets show accounting profits but after investment, cash flows may be negative. If advantage not built before growth slows, profits disappear.

### Competitive Advantage Sustainability
Advantage is only sustainable if it cannot be replicated at acceptable cost. Three paths to sustainability:
1. Unique resource possession (patents, unique assets)
2. Scarce resource / critical mass economics (scale, experience, network effects)
3. Complex capabilities embedded in organization (hard to understand, buy, or replicate in time)
